Practical Excel Work — Bank Reconciliation

I use Microsoft Excel to organise transaction data, compare bank statement entries with accounting ledger records, identify unmatched items and prepare exception reports. This independent practice project demonstrates the reconciliation approach and Excel techniques relevant to my finance operations experience. The sample data is fictional and does not contain employer or customer information.

Step 1 · Data preparation

Organise source data

Keep bank statement and ledger data in separate structured tables with transaction date, reference number, narration, debit, credit and net amount.

Step 2 · Matching

Compare transactions

Use transaction reference and net amount to compare bank entries against ledger entries. Review transaction dates and value dates as part of the check.

Step 3 · Exception handling

Identify differences

Flag unmatched bank entries, ledger-only entries and duplicate matches. Investigate bank charges, returns, timing differences and missing postings.

Step 4 · Reporting

Prepare reconciliation output

Use filters, status columns and conditional formatting to separate matched transactions from exceptions and focus follow-up on open items.

Excel functions and features demonstrated

  • Excel Tables
  • COUNTIFS
  • INDEX / MATCH
  • IFERROR
  • IF
  • Filters
  • Conditional Formatting
  • Formula-based exception flags
Portfolio practice result: The fictional sample demonstrates 8 matched bank entries, 2 bank-side exceptions and 2 ledger-only entries. In a live reconciliation, each exception would be checked against supporting documents and resolved or carried forward with a reason.

This is an independent demonstration workbook, not a claim that the sample transactions belong to my employer. It illustrates how I structure an Excel reconciliation and investigate differences.
